House of Living Heritage

Our Purpose

Structure around
continuity

House of Living Heritage exists to strengthen living heritage systems shaped by the Himalayan Silk Route continuum and the communities that sustain them. We believe heritage survives not by being preserved in isolation, but by remaining lived, adaptive, and economically viable.

Across mountain landscapes and historic exchange corridors, traditions in craft, food, music, architecture, and ecological knowledge evolved through movement, trade, and intergenerational transmission. HLH responds by building structure around continuity.

Our Vision

Enduring cultural institutions where living heritage can travel across landscapes, generations, and contemporary life — remaining deeply rooted in people, place, and inherited knowledge.

Our Mission

To strengthen and carry forward living heritage through long-term cultural platforms that integrate documentation, creative economies, cultural landscapes, archives, and heritage-led experiences.

What We Build

Interconnected Systems for
a Living Heritage

HLH builds long-term cultural systems that sustain heritage, empower communities, and create meaningful value across generations.

01

Interconnected Cultural Systems

Platforms that link tradition-bearing communities, creative practice, and public understanding across sectors and scales.

02

Documentary & Cultural Media

Cinematic and archival storytelling that carries nuance, consent, and long-form narrative into global audiences.

03

Craft & Creative Economies

Market design, training, and guidance that keep artisan livelihoods central to heritage-led regional development.

04

Food, Culinary & Music Systems

Documentation, convening, and distribution models that keep gastronomic and sonic heritage active in economy and ritual.

05

Cultural Houses & Built Heritage

Planning and governance for spaces where craft, gathering, and transmission can remain physically rooted and well maintained.

06

Heritage Landscapes & Responsible Travel

Corridor planning that balances visitor economies with ecological limits and host community agency along cultural routes.

07

Environmental & Climate Heritage

Field programmes that align ecological knowledge, stewardship ethics, and climate adaptation with cultural continuity.

08

Ritual, Festival & Social Practice

Protocols and partnerships that protect the dignity of ceremonial life while enabling thoughtful participation and documentation.

09

Archival & Knowledge Systems

Repositories, metadata standards, and access policies that treat knowledge as a living commons rather than a static vault.

Culture does not survive as memory alone. It survives through people, movement, practice, adaptation, and continuity.

Where We Work

Our Geographic Lens

HLH's regional focus is cultural rather than political. We recognize heritage as a living network flowing across mountains, corridors, and diaspora landscapes.

Mountain Cultural Zones

Himalaya · Karakoram · Hindu Kush

High-altitude landscapes forming a shared ecological and cultural continuum — pashmina and wool traditions, vernacular architecture, seasonal food systems, oral histories, and craft rooted in material ecology.

  • Kashmir
  • Gilgit-Baltistan
  • Hunza
  • Ladakh

Historic Silk Route Corridors

Trans-Regional Exchange Networks

Trade, pilgrimage, and migration routes that enabled sustained cultural interaction — circulating craft techniques, culinary traditions, musical styles, and architectural forms across mountain passes and valleys.

South Asian Cultural Sphere

Shared Aesthetic & Knowledge Traditions

Long-standing regional interaction across ritual and culinary systems, poetic and musical traditions, artisan networks, and social festival practices.

Global Diaspora Hubs

Cultural Circulation Beyond Origin

Diaspora communities sustain and reinterpret cultural practices across new landscapes — with collaboration hubs in Dubai, London, New York, Toronto, Riyadh, Doha, and beyond.
